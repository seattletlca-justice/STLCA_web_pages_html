      How to read these maps

      • One dot is one complaint record. Several complaints from one address sit on top of each other; click a dot to see the others at that address.
      • Left map: housing conditions. Right map: paperwork and landlord-tenant law. A complaint that names both appears on both maps. The two maps move together.
      • The light shapes are the seven council districts (outlines from the City’s council district file); blue-gray is water or outside the city. District __N__ has the dark outline. The map has no street tiles, so industrial areas with few homes, such as the Duwamish valley, show no gray dots but are still land.
      • District __N__ is in full color; the rest of the city is faded context. Click a faded dot to jump to that district’s page for the details.
      • Zoom in to street level to see buildings. Each building that has complaints is drawn from the City’s building outlines (2023, from the open data portal). The darker the building, the more complaints on that map are at it, counting only what passes the filters and the ticked groups. Hover over a building for its count and addresses; click it to open its newest complaint.
      • The color is the problem named in the description. A complaint that names several problems in one map takes the first matching color in that map’s list, and the card lists all of them.
      • 3-day notices are found by wording. The description or the City’s staff note (DESCRIPTION1) says “3-day notice” or “three day notice” (or a 3-day pay, comply or vacate). It can be an eviction notice or another kind, such as a notice of entry or repairs. The card shows the phrase and which field it came from.
      • Just cause eviction (JCEO) comes from the City’s JCEO label or from “just cause” wording. The JCEO label is a staff code, usually in DESCRIPTION1 (some are typed in the description). The card says which one a dot is based on. Many of these complaints also name an eviction, a rent increase or a lease problem. They show the JCEO color (only a 3-day notice is listed ahead of it); untick JCEO to see their other color.
      • Unassigned (black) means no problem could be assigned from the description. It does not mean there was no problem.
      • The other problem types come from the rules in the vocabulary audit. Borderline wording is left out, so a missing color is not a finding either way.
      • The City label (POTA, HOUSING and others) is staff-entered, in DESCRIPTION1. The filter lets you compare it with the map. Apart from JCEO it never colors a dot.
      • “Inspection date in the record” is a data flag. It shows whether the City’s export has an inspection date for the complaint. It is not proof that an inspection did or did not happen.
