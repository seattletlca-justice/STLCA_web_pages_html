Two roles, held apart

STLCA develops the public-facing analysis, editorial stories, findings and accountability recommendations. Seattle R.A.R.E. protects the evidence trail behind that work. Keeping the two separate is what makes the work checkable.

STLCA

Seattle Tenant-Landlord Compliance & Accountability

Develops the analysis, the findings, the editorial framing and the practical accountability recommendations that follow from documented gaps.

Seattle R.A.R.E.

Records Accountability & Review Engine

Preserves source records, defines the record universe, documents methodology, separates fact from analysis, and keeps public claims bounded by what the evidence supports.

What this is not

STLCA is not an enforcement agency and is not an authority. It issues no orders, levies no fines, compels no compliance, and makes no legal findings of guilt or liability. Where older material in the archive uses the word "Authority" in the organisation's name, that usage is superseded — it implies a government body with enforcement power that STLCA's charter explicitly disclaims.

The purpose is public accountability: make the record reviewable, identify documented gaps, state limitations plainly, and present feasible controls. The current direction on solutions is to strengthen traceability, reporting, QA and public visibility within existing systems rather than treating the creation of a new agency as the default answer.

Publication standards

  • No public number without provenance. Source snapshot, record universe, date scope, extraction date, QA status, exclusions and limitations travel with every figure.
  • Facts, analysis and allegations stay separate. Documented fact, reported statement, calculation, inference, allegation, finding and limitation are not blended into one claim.
  • Missing data is not proof of non-occurrence.
  • Failed QA does not publish.
  • Public record, public name. A person is named only when the name appears in a public record, and the name stays attached to the record it came from.
  • Generated is not delivered. Design mockup content is not automatically approved content, and figures are not copied from mockups.

Public posture

Civic. Evidence-based. Transparent. Bounded. Independently reviewable.

Editorial frame

Seattle's Council passed Council Bill 121254, expanding renter-protection enforcement tools and responsibilities. The question that follows is not whether renters should be protected. It is how the public knows that expanded authority is being used consistently, completely and as intended — and what records would show it either way.

The related framing question, "if records are required to prove landlord compliance, what records prove government enforcement," is an accountability analogy. It is not a claim that landlord evidentiary rules legally apply to SDCI.

Don't take our word for it. Follow the record. Every figure on this site traces to a named file with a published checksum. The methodology explains how to reproduce it, and Reports & Source Records holds the underlying evidence.
