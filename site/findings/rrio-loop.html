Feature story

It is presented alongside the findings because it bears on the same question, not because it carries the same evidentiary weight. It has its own universe, its own matching step, and its own limitations.

Tenant-reported conditions at certified properties

RRIO — the Rental Registration and Inspection Ordinance — certifies that a rental property met a minimum standard at the time it was inspected. These are the tenant-reported condition categories recorded at properties that matched an RRIO certification, over the same period.

These are different measures A certification records a point-in-time result against a defined checklist. A complaint records what a tenant reported at some later date. Neither is a proxy for the other, and the gap between them is the subject of this story rather than its conclusion.
What this supports

That tenant-reported conditions continued to be recorded at properties carrying RRIO certification, and that a documented inspection is recorded for a minority of those complaints.

What this does not support

That RRIO certification was improperly issued, that certified properties are unsafe, or that any specific property failed an obligation. The match is address-level, and an address is not a finding.

Why this is a story and not a finding

The three core findings rest on field states inside one frozen extract. This analysis joins two different record systems — code complaints and rental registrations — on address. Address matching introduces its own error, in both directions: a certified property whose address is written differently across systems will not match, and a matched address may cover a building whose certification and complaint relate to different units or different years.

That is a real limitation, not a hedge. It is why the figures here are described as matched complaints rather than as complaints at certified properties, and why this page carries a feature label rather than a finding number.
