The Record

If records are required to prove landlord compliance,
what records prove government enforcement?

Seattle's Council passed Council Bill 121254, expanding renter-protection enforcement tools and responsibilities. That creates one accountability question: when government gives an agency more authority to protect renters, how does the public know that authority is actually being used consistently, completely, and as intended?

This is an accountability analogy, not a claim that landlord evidentiary rules legally apply to SDCI.

What this is

STLCA uses public records to examine whether Seattle's housing-enforcement systems can be reconstructed and evaluated from complaint through investigation, inspection or finding, correction, closure, and reporting. Seattle R.A.R.E. — the Records Accountability & Review Engine — protects the evidence trail behind that work by preserving source records, defining the record universe, documenting methodology, separating facts from analysis, and keeping public claims bounded by what the evidence actually supports.

This site is not a replacement enforcement agency. STLCA does not issue orders, levy fines, compel compliance, or make legal findings of guilt or liability. Its purpose is public accountability: make the record reviewable, identify documented gaps, state limitations, and present feasible controls.

How to read anything here

  1. See the question.
  2. Open a finding or story.
  3. Inspect the supporting record.
  4. Review what the evidence establishes.
  5. Review what it does not establish.
  6. Follow methodology and source records.
  7. Reach an independent conclusion.
Missing data is not proof of non-occurrence A blank inspection field does not prove that an inspection never occurred. A closed status does not by itself prove correction. A report not located in the searched public record is not proof that the report never existed elsewhere.
